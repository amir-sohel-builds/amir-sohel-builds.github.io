Anatomy of an agentic platform

October 2026

People ask what an agentic platform actually is, underneath the pitch. Here is the shape of the one I run, layer by layer. The names change from company to company. The layers do not.

Model access. A clean way to reach the models, in our case the Claude API and Amazon Bedrock, with routing, quotas, and cost visibility. If every team wires up its own model access, you get sprawl and surprise bills.

Runtime. Where agents actually execute: sessions, memory, retries, timeouts. Agents fail in boring ways, and the runtime is what keeps a boring failure from becoming an outage.

Tool gateway. Agents need to touch real systems: tickets, dashboards, internal data. We connect them through MCP and a gateway, so every tool call is authenticated, authorized, and logged. An agent with unlogged access to production systems is a liability, not a feature.

Evaluation pipeline. Offline evals before release, online checks after. Quality is a number you watch, not a feeling.

Observability. Traces, decisions, and costs, per agent. When leadership asks what the platform did this week, this layer answers.

Identity and guardrails. Who the agent acts as, what it may do without a human, and where it must stop and ask. This layer is the difference between automation and risk.

None of these layers is glamorous. Together they are the difference between a demo and a platform a company can bet on.
